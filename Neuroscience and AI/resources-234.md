### 🤖 September 2026 hottest on record for contiguous US

September 2026 set a new temperature record for the contiguous US. The month averaged 69.42 °F, about 4.5 °F above the historic 20th‑century average. January‑September 2026 were the hottest first nine months since at least 1895. The data come from NOAA.

Key Points:

- **September 2026 average temperature**: 69.42 °F (20.79 °C)

- January‑September 2026 were the hottest first nine months on record since at least 1895

### 📊 Cincinnati Cohort Biomarker Program study links activity to slower PD progression

The Cincinnati Cohort Biomarker Program analyzed data from 294 people with mild-to-moderate Parkinson's. The study found that higher levels of physical activity were associated with slower cognitive and motor progression in Parkinson's disease.

Key Points:

- **Participants**: 294 mild-to-moderate Parkinson's patients

- **Finding**: Higher physical activity linked to slower cognitive and motor progression

### 🤖 US proposes $100K fee for international OPT work program

The Trump administration announced a proposal that would charge $100,000 for international graduates to work in the United States under Optional Practical Training (OPT). Universities would have to pay $70,000 for the first year of OPT and an additional $30,000 for a two‑year STEM extension. The rule is open for public comment until 9 November.

Key Points:

- **Fee**: $70,000 for the first year of OPT, plus $30,000 for a two‑year STEM extension, totaling $100,000 per graduate.

- **Impact**: DHS estimates OPT enrollment could fall up to 44%, costing universities $8.4‑$16.5 billion annually.

### 🚀 Microblazar detection in Milky Way

Microblazars are miniature versions of blazars. Astronomers say they have identified a microblazar in the Milky Way. The object could explain the origins of some ultra‑high‑energy particles.

Key Points:

- **Search**: Astronomers have spent decades searching for a miniature version of a cosmic powerhouse.

- **Detection**: They may have found a microblazar in the Milky Way.

- **Impact**: Microblazars may be responsible for mysterious ultra‑high‑energy particles.
