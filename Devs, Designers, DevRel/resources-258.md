### 🤖 Technical Writing and Senior Software Engineer Guidelines

#### Introduction
Guidelines for transforming Twitter threads and LinkedIn posts into high-quality, professional technical markdown articles. The guidelines cover banned words, senior-engineer tone, sentence variance, and no hype.

#### Key Points:
• Banned words include "delve", "testament", "tapestry", "unlock", "unlocking", "seamless", "game-changer", "revolutionary", "groundbreaking", "moreover", "furthermore", "in conclusion", "shines a light", "treasure trove", "leverage", "robust", "key takeaway", "elevate", "cutting-edge", "beacon", "look no further", "significant", "significantly", "significant shifts", "making waves", "next-gen", "wild", "sophisticated", "most powerful", "signaling", "broader reach", "push boundaries", "pushing boundaries", "extensibility", "masterclass", "paving the way", "incredible ways", "blurring lines", "deep dive", "supercharge", "supercharged", "supercharging", "paradigm shift", "synergy", "plethora", "myriad", "harness", "harnessing", "unleash", "unleashing".
• Zero 3rd-person meta introductions, starting immediately with the core technical subject, architecture, or benchmark.
• No marketing fluff, avoiding empty hype adjectives.
• Human senior-engineer tone, writing as if sharing what actually works directly with another senior engineer.
• Sentence variance, using a natural human rhythm with short, punchy 4-to-6-word statements and slightly longer technical explanations.
• CLI / TOOL FOCUS, referring to CLI tools, utilities, or scripts strictly as CLI tools, utilities, or scripts.
