### 🚨 UUIDs - A Bad Idea?

Are UUIDs a bad idea in 2026? UUIDs (Guid in C#) are widely used as unique database identifiers. UUIDs are "random", which makes them popular in distributed systems because there's a low chance of collisions. However, UUIDs have two big drawbacks: 1. UUIDs slow down database queries. 2. UUIDs are not secure. This highlights the limitations of UUIDs as a unique identifier.

Key Points:

- **UUIDs**: UUIDs are widely used as unique database identifiers, but they have significant drawbacks.

- **Database Queries**: UUIDs can slow down database queries, making them less efficient.

- **Security**: UUIDs are not secure, making them vulnerable to attacks.

- **Alternatives**: Alternative unique identifiers, such as auto-incrementing integers, may be more suitable for certain use cases.
