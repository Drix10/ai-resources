### 📊 Continuous Audio Thinking for Large Audio Language Models adds latent workspace

Continuous Audio Thinking (CoAT) introduces a continuous latent workspace for organizing acoustic information before response generation. CoAT is processed in a single prefill and does not add autoregressive decoding cost. Benchmarks on Qwen2-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, and Audio Flamingo 3 show performance gains across audio reasoning, understanding, music classification, speech emotion, and transcription.

Key Points:

- Continuous thinking block processed in a single prefill, no additional autoregressive decoding cost.

- **Gains reported on three LALMs**: Qwen2-Audio, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, and Audio Flamingo 3 across a broad benchmark suite.

🔗 Resources:

- [Original post](https://x.com/ArxivSound/status/2114702114547220813)

### 🤖 Why Full-Duplex Speech Models Collapse in Long Conversations

Why Full-Duplex Speech Models Collapse in Long Conversations presents a new benchmark for long-duration dialogue. It shows that existing full-duplex models degrade or exhaust context within minutes and proposes two fixes that lower collapse in hour-long replays.

Key Points:

- Full-Duplex Long-Horizon Benchmark (FD-LHB) evaluates five recent models on long replayed conversations and live interaction with an LLM partner.

- Initialization-Anchor Pinning (IAP) and Duplex Repetition Penalty (DRP) reduce conversational collapse in replays up to an hour without retraining.

### 📊 Noisy Target Training Meets Drifting for Weakly-Supervised Speech Enhancement

Noisy Target Training Meets Drifting for Weakly-Supervised Speech Enhancement adds a Sinkhorn drifting loss to NyTT. The loss aligns enhanced speech distribution with unpaired clean speech in latent space. Experiments on VoiceBank-DEMAND and CHiME-3 real recordings demonstrate the combination’s efficacy.

### 🤖 X-Dubbing: Speech-to-Speech Translation under Temporal and Visual Constraints announced

X-Dubbing introduces a joint time-aware speech-to-text translation and video-aware text-to-speech system for cross-lingual dubbing. The method consists of a Dubbing Translator that creates temporally structured scripts and a Voice Actor that renders them in sync with the source video. It requires no paired dubbing data, training on speech-text and monolingual audio-visual data respectively.

Key Points:

- Dubbing Translator generates temporally structured scripts.

- Voice Actor renders scripts synchronized with the source video using a language-agnostic timing budget.
