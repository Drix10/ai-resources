### 🧬 Bioengineering - TNF-α Binder Design

TNF-α is a protein that plays a key role in immune responses, but its continuous production in the joint or gut wall can lead to diseases like rheumatoid arthritis and Crohn's disease. A TNF-α binder is a protein that can recognize and bind to TNF-α, potentially reducing its activity and alleviating symptoms. The design of a TNF-α binder is a complex task, requiring a deep understanding of protein structure and function.

Key Points:

- **TNF-α Structure**: TNF-α is a homotrimer, consisting of three identical chains that form one complex. Each chain has a receptor-binding site that sits in the groove between two neighboring chains.

- **Epitope Recognition**: The TNF-α binder must recognize and bind to the epitope, which is the specific region on the surface of the TNF-α protein that is recognized by the immune system. The epitope must be shared between human and mouse TNF-α to allow for testing in mouse models.

- **Design Constraints**: The design of the TNF-α binder must take into account the constraints of the epitope recognition and the need to bind to both human and mouse TNF-α. This requires a careful balance between specificity and cross-reactivity.

### 🧬 Bioengineering - TNF-α Binder Design Challenge

The TNF-α binder design challenge is an open competition that allows anyone to participate. The challenge requires the design of a de novo TNF-α binder that can recognize and bind to TNF-α. The binder must be 10-250 amino acids in length, with up to 40 amino acids for Track 1, up to 20 amino acids for Tracks 2 and 3. The challenge uses a Claude-based workflow to select the best designs, which are then produced and tested in an automated lab.
