### 🚨 Anthropic paper shows 0.1% poisoning plants RCE backdoor

Anthropic “Agentic Backdoors via Pretraining Poisoning” demonstrates that poisoning just 0.1% of pretraining data can plant a remote-code-execution backdoor in an AI agent. It also notes that a modern frontier model lab would need to train on roughly 750 billion poisoned words.

Key Points:

- Poisoning just 0.1% of pretraining data can plant a remote-code-execution backdoor in an AI agent.

- Training a modern frontier model lab would have to train on ~750,000,000,000 poisoned words.

### 🤖 Holon-S1 predicts genes from slides, beats models

Holon-S1 predicts genes and biomarkers from routine pathology slides and beats two other models at predicting spatial gene activity. It has identified potentially novel biomarkers in breast and colon cancer and was evaluated across 16 organ types and more than 15 scanners using both FFPE and fresh-frozen tissue.

Key Points:

- Beats two other models at predicting spatial gene activity in a controlled comparison

- Evaluated across 16 organ types and more than 15 scanners, using slides from both formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded and fresh-frozen tissue

### 🤖 L3G-World memory grows with scene, 9 fps real-time

L3G-World is a world model using 3D Gaussians that hold VAE latents and renders directly into a video model’s latent space at 9 fps. Memory expands as new parts of the scene are explored while keeping previously built Gaussians. Code release is announced.

Key Points:

- Memory grows with the scene, not clip length

- Real-time performance at 9 fps

### 🤖 AI agents exchange secret word using air-writing, Morse, semaphore, binary

AI agents exchanged a secret word using air-writing, Morse code, flag semaphore and a 5-bit binary alphabet, without pre-agreed protocol. The experiment shows agents can recognize creative communication solutions generated by other AIs.

Key Points:

- Two isolated copies of the same AI exchanged a secret word using only a robot and a camera.

- They succeeded with air-writing, Morse code, flag semaphore and a 5-bit binary alphabet, without agreeing on a protocol beforehand.

### 🤖 Song Exploder – open-source Mac tool for stem separation and mashups

Song Exploder is now open-source and runs on a Mac. It separates a song into vocals, drums, bass and chords, generates MIDI, detects key and tempo, and can place one song’s vocals over another song’s beat. Licensed under MIT.

Key Points:

- Drop any song and hear vocals, drums, bass and chords on their own.

- Writes MIDI, finds the key and tempo, and puts one song’s vocals over another song’s beat.
