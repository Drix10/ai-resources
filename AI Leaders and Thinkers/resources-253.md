### Latency Benchmarking: The Hidden Coordination Problem

Latency benchmarking is a crucial aspect of system performance evaluation, but it often comes with a hidden coordination problem. The most popular way to measure latency is to wait for each response before sending the next request, which inadvertently coordinates with the system being measured. This can lead to inaccurate results, especially when dealing with systems that have garbage collection or other background processes.

Key Points:

- **Garbage Collection Coordination**: The coordination problem arises from the fact that garbage collection can pause the system, causing the latency benchmark to wait for the pause to complete before sending the next request.

- **Inaccurate Results**: This coordination can lead to inaccurate results, as the benchmark is not truly measuring the system's performance under normal conditions.

- **Trade-offs**: The trade-off between accuracy and coordination is a delicate one, and developers must carefully consider the implications of their benchmarking approach.


Actionable Takeaway:
- **Use Asynchronous Benchmarking**: Consider using asynchronous benchmarking techniques that can measure latency without coordinating with the system being measured.
