### 📊 High-fidelity entanglement of two spectrally-distinct solid state emitters achieves C=0.95

High-fidelity entanglement of two spectrally-distinct solid state emitters achieves a concurrence of 0.95 using narrowband photon inputs. The method uses readily available entangled photon pairs to entangle emitters with large transition frequency separations and linewidth variations, removing the need for identical emitters.

Key Points:

- Concurrence C = 0.95 for typical quantum dots.

- Entangles emitters with large frequency and linewidth differences using entangled photon inputs.

### 🤖 Can Entanglement-Induced Feature-Map Designs Improve Projected Quantum Kernels?

Can Entanglement-Induced Feature-Map Designs Improve Projected Quantum Kernels? introduces quantum feature maps that use two-qubit entanglement to capture multivariate correlations in IoT data. The paper isolates native inductive bias by comparing untuned and tuned projected quantum kernels against an RBF baseline. Results indicate that the proposed entanglement topologies exhibit a significantly stronger native inductive bias than standard designs.

Key Points:

- Untuned and tuned projected quantum kernels are benchmarked against an RBF baseline to expose native inductive bias.

- Entanglement-based feature maps show a significantly stronger native inductive bias than conventional designs.
