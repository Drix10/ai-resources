### 🚀 Drones in Swarms: The Difference Between a Real Swarm and a Light Show

Drones in swarms are often misunderstood. Most people think of a swarm as a group of drones following a single leader. However, this is not how real swarms work. In a real swarm, each drone is an individual entity that makes its own decisions based on its environment and the behavior of its neighbors.

Key Points:


- **


- **Swarm Intelligence**: A real swarm is a complex system where individual drones interact with each other and their environment to achieve a common goal. This is different from a light show, where drones are controlled by a single leader.

- **Decentralized Decision-Making**: In a real swarm, each drone makes its own decisions based on its local environment and the behavior of its neighbors. This allows the swarm to adapt to changing conditions and respond to threats.

- **Scalability**: Real swarms can be much larger than light shows, with hundreds or even thousands of drones working together to achieve a common goal.

### 🚀 Durable Agents: A Fresh Take on Architecture

The traditional approach to building durable agents is to focus on individual components and their interactions. However, this can lead to a fragmented and brittle system. A fresh take on architecture is to focus on the overall system and its behavior, rather than individual components.

Key Points:


- **


- **System-Centric Approach**: A fresh take on architecture focuses on the overall system and its behavior, rather than individual components. This allows for a more holistic understanding of the system and its interactions.

- **Component Interactions**: By focusing on the interactions between components, rather than individual components, we can build more durable and resilient systems.

- **Co-Presenting Architecture**: Co-presenting a shared architecture and then diving into each component allows for a more comprehensive understanding of the system.
