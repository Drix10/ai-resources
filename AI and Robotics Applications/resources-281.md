### 🤖 Reflex humanoid control catches objects without falling, 1.6M rendered steps

Reflex, a Visually Grounded Reactive Humanoid Control system, lets a Unitree G1 catch boxes thrown by people using only an onboard RGB-D camera. The robot repositions its feet and torso, forms a support with its forearms and chest, absorbs momentum and stays upright. Training runs in three stages in Isaac Lab and requires about 1.6 million rendered steps.

Key Points:

- Reflex enables a Unitree G1 to catch boxes with an RGB-D camera, repositioning feet and torso and using forearms and chest for support.

- Training consists of three Isaac Lab stages and uses roughly 1.6 million rendered steps, with a latent recovered from 25 frames of egocentric RGB-D history.
