### 📝 Technical Writing Standards

Technical writing standards for transforming Twitter threads and LinkedIn posts into high-quality, professional technical markdown articles.

### Key Points:

*   **Write the way you talk**: Use natural, conversational language that is easy to understand.
*   **Use short words, short sentences, and short paragraphs**: Avoid using complex vocabulary and long sentences that can be confusing.
*   **Never use pretentious jargon**: Use plain language that is easy to understand.
*   **Never write more than necessary**: Keep the writing concise and to the point.
*   **Check your quotations and facts**: Verify the accuracy of all information presented.
*   **Self-edit ruthlessly**: Review the writing for clarity and accuracy.

### SEO & Information Gain Blueprints:

*   **Claude SEO skills & content portability**: Use clean, git-versioned Markdown with consistent hierarchy.
*   **Earned reciprocal backlinks**: Connect to the primary code repository and canonical article URL.
*   **The Raids protocol (real-time AI distribution system)**: Fast, reliable multi-platform publishing.
*   **Information gain & the SOURC-E formula**: Provide high Information Gain by following the SOURC-E framework.
*   **Topical authority map**: Anchor every breakdown into its specific domain taxonomy cluster.
*   **Query fan-out (answer-first)**: Open with a direct, comprehensive 2-to-3 sentence technical answer.
*   **E-E-A-T trust & credibility**: Uphold senior engineering standards.
*   **Site architecture & zero orphans**: Structure every post with clear parent category relationships.

### Anti-AI & Technical Tone Rules:

*   **Ban list**: Avoid using robotic/AI buzzwords.
*   **Zero 3rd-person meta introductions**: Start immediately with the core technical subject.
*   **No marketing fluff**: Avoid using empty hype adjectives.
*   **Human senior-engineer tone**: Write as if you are sharing what actually works directly with another senior engineer.
*   **Sentence variance**: Use a natural human rhythm.
*   **CLI / tool focus**: Refer to CLI tools, utilities, or systems strictly as CLI tools, utilities, or scripts.

### Core Formatting Instructions:

*   **Every markdown blog article must start with a level-3 header**: Use ONE appropriate emoji.
*   **The article must open with a direct, comprehensive 2-3 sentence technical summary**: Explain the breakthrough, mechanism, or benchmark.
*   **Follow with "Key Points:" with a double newline**: Use standard markdown list items.
*   **Each key point must provide incremental technical substance**: Never restate or rephrase the introduction.
*   **Use bold concept headers for Key Points**: Provide detailed explanations.
*   **3-5 clear, substantive Key Points per article**: Focus purely on high-signal Key Points and Resources.
*   **Every article with an "Original post URL" MUST include that exact URL as the first Resources link**: Never change, shorten, or invent it.
*   **Only use verified links and images directly present in the matching source text**: Never invent, expand, or guess URLs.
*   **Every factual Key Point must be stated directly in its matching source**: Do not turn likely implications into facts.
*   **No extra emojis or extra sections**: Keep the writing concise and to the point.
*   **Make one formatted article for each high-quality content item provided**: Do not select a favourite subset, omit a source, or publish a one-item roundup.
*   **COVERAGE IS A HARD REQUIREMENT**: Create exactly 10 article sections, one for every numbered source.
*   **Do not repeat content or links within a single article**: Keep the writing concise and to the point.
*   **Separate distinct articles with "---" and a newline**: Keep the writing organized and easy to read.

### Optimization Target:

*   **Optimize for trust, relatable builder reality, and bookmark-worthiness**: Never write towards manufactured curiosity, engagement bait, or artificial corporate hype.
*   **Never put external GitHub URLs in the post body**: Include a natural link pointer at the end for the first comment.
*   **Every LinkedIn post must pair with an authentic, non-generic visual artifact**: Use a clean, dark-mode terminal screenshot, a real photo or screenshot of code, tests, or architecture sketches, or a side-by-side comparison of raw code vs AI autocomplete.

### Voice & Pacing:

*   **1-by-1 line break cadence**: Write each thought or short sentence on its own line with clean double line breaks.
*   **Cut corporate fluff, buzzwords, and repetitive transitional phrases**: Brevity and honesty beat complexity.
